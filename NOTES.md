# Notes on this English Odyssey

A short guide to the English in this repository. It follows that English only. The Greek behind it is Perseus `tlg0012.tlg002.perseus-grc2` (Allen, by way of the 1919 Murray Loeb Greek). No other English Odyssey was used as a source.

Line numbers match the Greek. This edition has 12,107 verses. It omits the plus-verses 10.456, 16.101, and 23.49.

## This English

Clear spoken English, one line per Greek line. Epithets are kept as meaning (owl-eyed Athena, Odysseus of many devices, careful Penelope). A few Greek words are left visible where English has no honest single word: *lycabas*, *atta*, *sardonic*, Bad-Ilium not to be named, Nobody / nobody for Οὖτις / μή τις.

At 11.134–137 and again at 23.281–82, Teiresias says death will come from the sea. The Greek can also mean away from the sea. This edition keeps “from the sea.”

The hanging of the twelve women and the mutilation of Melanthius are not softened. Amphimedon’s story in Book 24 is his compression, not rewritten to match Books 19–23.

## Book I

The Muse is asked for the man of many turnings. All the others who escaped Troy are home. Odysseus alone is held by Calypso. Zeus names reckless doing and Aegisthus; Athena wins leave to act. She goes to Ithaca as Mentes, puts force in Telemachus, and sends him after news of his father. Penelope stops Phemius’s song of the bitter homecoming. Telemachus claims the house and calls an assembly for dawn.

## Book II

The Ithacan assembly. Telemachus names the suitors’ eating of the house. Antinous blames Penelope’s web. Two eagles tear each other over the meeting; Halitherses reads death for the suitors. Mentor shames the people. Athena as Mentor outfits a ship. Eurycleia swears not to tell Penelope until the eleventh or twelfth day. They sail at night for Pylos.

## Book III

Telemachus and Athena (as Mentor) reach Pylos at a sacrifice to Poseidon. Nestor tells the quarrel of the Atreidai, the scattered homecomings, and Agamemnon’s death. He cannot say where Odysseus is. Athena leaves as a sea-eagle. Nestor sends Telemachus overland with Peisistratus to Menelaus in Sparta.

## Book IV

They find Menelaus celebrating a double wedding. Helen names Telemachus. Menelaus tells of Egypt, Proteus, Ajax’s death, Agamemnon’s murder, and Odysseus held on Calypso’s island. In Ithaca the suitors learn of the voyage and plot to ambush Telemachus in the strait. Penelope hears and weeps. Athena sends a phantom of Iphthime to comfort her.

## Book V

The gods send Hermes to Ogygia. Calypso must let Odysseus go. She helps him build a raft. On the eighteenth day Poseidon wrecks him in sight of Scheria. Ino gives him her veil. He swims two days and comes ashore at a river mouth, then sleeps under two olives.

## Book VI

Athena sends Nausicaa to wash clothes at the river. Odysseus wakes, covers himself, and asks her help. She gives him clothing and food and tells him to follow her wagon to the city, then sit in the ashes at Arete’s knees.

## Book VII

Athena as a girl leads him to Alcinous’s house. He clasps Arete’s knees. They feast him. He will not name himself yet. He tells how he came from Calypso and the wreck. They promise an escort home.

## Book VIII

Games and a feast. Demodocus sings the quarrel of Odysseus and Achilles, then Ares and Aphrodite, then the wooden horse. Odysseus weeps. Alcinous asks who he is.

## Book IX

Odysseus names himself and tells: the Cicones; the Lotus-eaters; the Cyclops. He is Nobody. They blind Polyphemus and escape under the sheep. Odysseus calls out his real name. Polyphemus prays to Poseidon.

## Book X

Aeolus’s bag of winds; the companions open it in sight of Ithaca. The Laestrygonians destroy all ships but one. On Aeaea Circe drugs the men into swine. Hermes gives Odysseus moly. He beds Circe after she swears. A year of meat and wine. She sends him to Hades to ask Teiresias. Elpenor dies falling from the roof.

## Book XI

The pit of blood. Elpenor asks for burial. Teiresias: leave Helios’s cattle unharmed; kill the suitors; walk inland with an oar until a man calls it a winnowing-fan; death will come from the sea. Anticleia, the heroines, Agamemnon, Achilles, Ajax (who will not speak), the punished dead, Heracles. Odysseus cuts the story for sleep, then goes on.

## Book XII

They bury Elpenor. Circe maps the road: Sirens, the Rocks, Scylla and Charybdis, Thrinacia. They pass the Sirens (Odysseus bound); Scylla takes six men. On Thrinacia Eurylochus drives the killing of Helios’s cattle. Zeus wrecks the ship. Odysseus alone reaches Calypso.

## Book XIII

The Phaeacians give gifts and sail him sleeping to Ithaca. Poseidon turns their ship to stone. Odysseus wakes and does not know the land. Athena as a shepherd, then as herself, hides the gifts, disguises him as an old beggar, and sends him to the swineherd. She will fetch Telemachus from Sparta.

## Book XIV

Odysseus at Eumaeus’s steading. He tells a Cretan lie. Eumaeus has kept faith for years and will not believe Odysseus is near. They eat a hog and sleep.

## Book XV

Athena tells Telemachus to sail home and to go first to the swineherd. Helen gives him a robe for his wedding. Theoclymenus the seer ships with him. Eumaeus tells Odysseus how he was stolen as a child. Telemachus lands and sends the ship to the city.

## Book XVI

Father and son meet at the steading. Athena restores Odysseus’s form. They plan the suitors’ death. The ambush ship returns empty. The suitors argue; Penelope appears. Odysseus is a beggar again before Eumaeus comes back.

## Book XVII

Telemachus goes to the house and tells Penelope a cleaned version of the journey. Odysseus and Eumaeus walk to town. Melanthius kicks Odysseus. Argos the dog knows his master and dies. Odysseus begs among the suitors. Antinous throws a footstool. Penelope asks to see the stranger.

## Book XVIII

Irus the public beggar fights Odysseus and is beaten. Amphinomus gets a warning and does not take it. Athena rouses Penelope to appear; the suitors give gifts. Melantho insults Odysseus. Eurymachus throws a stool and hits the wine-pourer.

## Book XIX

After the suitors sleep, Odysseus and Telemachus take the arms from the hall. Penelope questions the beggar. He says he hosted Odysseus in Crete. She weeps. Eurycleia washes his feet and knows the scar; he silences her. Penelope tells the dream of the geese and the eagle, and the gates of horn and ivory. She will set the bow contest tomorrow.

## Book XX

Odysseus lies awake; Athena sheds sleep. Omens: a thunderclap, a grinding woman who prays the suitors dine their last. Theoclymenus sees the hall running with blood and leaves. The suitors keep on.

## Book XXI

Penelope brings Odysseus’s bow. Telemachus almost strings it, then stops at a look from his father. The suitors fail. Odysseus, still a beggar, strings it and shoots through the axes. He nods to Telemachus.

## Book XXII

The next arrow takes Antinous in the throat. Odysseus names himself. The killing of the suitors fills the hall. Telemachus hangs the twelve women. Melanthius is mutilated. Phemius and Medon are spared. Eurycleia would cry out in triumph; Odysseus stops her: it is unholy to boast over the slain.

## Book XXIII

Eurycleia wakes Penelope. She will not believe. The bed test: their bed is built on a living olive; Odysseus named that. They go to bed. He tells her the road still ahead, the oar and the men who do not know the sea. Dawn; he goes to the country to see Laertes. They will have a story ready if anyone asks about the slain.

## Book XXIV

Hermes leads the suitors’ souls. Achilles and Agamemnon talk; Amphimedon tells the slaughter his way. Odysseus tests Laertes in the orchard, then names the trees he was given as a boy. They eat. Rumor goes through town. Eupeithes leads a party for vengeance. Athena and Zeus stop the fighting. A sworn peace.
